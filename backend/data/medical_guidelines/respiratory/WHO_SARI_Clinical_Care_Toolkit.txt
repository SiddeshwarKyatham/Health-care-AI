Section 1: Screening, Triage, and Initial Recognition
Patients presenting with severe respiratory symptoms require immediate triage. Severe acute respiratory infection (SARI) is defined as an acute respiratory infection with history of fever or measured fever >= 38.0 C and cough, with onset within the last 10 days, requiring hospitalization.
Clinical parameters to assess at triage include respiratory rate, work of breathing, arterial oxygen saturation (SpO2), heart rate, and blood pressure.
For adults, tachypnea (respiratory rate >= 30 breaths/min) or severe respiratory distress indicates severe pneumonia and warrants immediate oxygen supplementation.

=== PAGE 2 ===
Section 2: Oxygen Therapy and Hypoxemia Management
Hypoxemia is defined as SpO2 < 90% in non-pregnant adults and SpO2 < 92-95% in pregnant patients.
Initiate nasal cannula oxygen therapy at 1-5 L/min for mild hypoxemia. For severe hypoxemia (SpO2 < 88% or severe respiratory distress), utilize a non-rebreather reservoir mask at 10-15 L/min.
Target SpO2 is >= 94% during resuscitation in non-COPD patients. In patients with chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure (e.g. COPD), target SpO2 is 88-92% to avoid hypercapnic narcosis.
Monitor capillary blood gases or arterial blood gases (ABG) when available.

Section 3: Antimicrobial Therapy for Community-Acquired Pneumonia
Empiric antimicrobial therapy should be administered within 1 hour of initial patient evaluation for severe pneumonia or sepsis.
Mild to Moderate CAP (Outpatient / General Ward):
- Oral Amoxicillin/Clavulanate 1g twice daily OR oral Azithromycin 500mg daily.
Severe CAP (ICU / High Dependency Unit):
- Intravenous Ceftriaxone 2g once daily PLUS Intravenous Azithromycin 500mg once daily.
- If Pseudomonas infection is suspected, substitute Ceftriaxone with Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5g IV Q6H or Cefepime 2g IV Q8H.
- Adjust renal dosing in patients with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2.

Section 4: Sepsis Recognition and Resuscitation in SARI
Septic shock secondary to severe pulmonary infection is characterized by persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) >= 65 mmHg and serum lactate > 2 mmol/L despite fluid resuscitation.
Resuscitation protocol:
- Administer 30 mL/kg isotonic crystalloid (Ringer's Lactate or 0.9% Normal Saline) within the first 3 hours.
- Initiate Norepinephrine infusion as the first-choice vasopressor if MAP remains < 65 mmHg following fluid loading.
- Avoid fluid overload in acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS); transition to conservative fluid management once hemodynamic stability is achieved.
