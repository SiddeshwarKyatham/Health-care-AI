Section 1: Diagnosis of Acute Decompensated Heart Failure
Acute heart failure (AHF) refers to rapid onset or worsening of symptoms and/or signs of heart failure requiring urgent evaluation and therapy.
Key Diagnostic Features:
- Symptoms: Dyspnea at rest or exertion, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, fatigue.
- Signs: Peripheral edema, elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP), hepatojugular reflux, pulmonary rales/crackles on auscultation, third heart sound (S3 gallop).
Laboratory Biomarkers:
- NT-proBNP >= 300 pg/mL or BNP >= 100 pg/mL in non-acute setting.
- In acute emergency setting, NT-proBNP >= 450 pg/mL (age < 55), >= 900 pg/mL (age 55-75), >= 1800 pg/mL (age > 75).

Section 4: Pharmacological Management of Acute Decompensated Heart Failure
Loop Diuretics:
- Intravenous Loop Diuretics (Furosemide 40-80 mg IV bolus) are the cornerstone of therapy for acute pulmonary congestion and fluid overload.
- Double the oral home diuretic dose if patient was taking chronic oral loop diuretics.
Vasodilator Therapy:
- Intravenous Nitroglycerin infusion (start at 10-20 mcg/min, titrate up) for patients with acute heart failure and Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP) > 110 mmHg.
- Avoid nitrates in patients with SBP < 90 mmHg or severe aortic stenosis.
Inotropic Support:
- Inotropic agents (Dobutamine 2.5-20 mcg/kg/min or Milrinone) are indicated in cardiogenic shock with severe hypotension (SBP < 90 mmHg) and signs of hypoperfusion (cold extremities, oliguria, elevated serum lactate).

Section 7: Non-Invasive Ventilation and Advanced Mechanical Support
Non-Invasive Positive Pressure Ventilation (NIPPV / CPAP / BiPAP):
- Indicated in cardiogenic pulmonary edema with tachypnea (respiratory rate > 25 breaths/min) and hypoxemia (SpO2 < 90%) to reduce respiratory distress and intubation rate.
- Start CPAP at 5-10 cm H2O or BiPAP at IPAP 10 cm H2O / EPAP 4 cm H2O.
- Monitor for hypotension secondary to decreased venous return.
